What "blockchain verified" actually means

When a credential shows as verified on YouthChain, the blockchain confirms one specific thing: that the exact file you uploaded existed, unaltered, at the time shown. It does not independently confirm that the school, employer, or institution you named as the issuer actually issued it — title, issuer, and year are entered by you, the same as on a paper certificate. Only a fingerprint of your file (a cryptographic hash) is ever written to the blockchain — never your name, your documents, or any other personal information. See the verification page itself for the same disclosure at the point you'd actually rely on it.

Who can see your data

  • You, always, via the app.
  • An employer you apply to sees your application, CV, and any messages you send them for that application — nothing else.
  • YouthChain admins can see platform data to operate, moderate, and secure the service, and to review employer verification documents.
  • YouthChain does not sell personal data to third parties.

Your choices

  • You must actively accept this policy (a required checkbox) before registering.
  • You can request deletion of your account and data from within the app, or by contacting YouthChain. This deletes your uploaded files (CVs, credentials, supporting documents) and your personal information (name, phone, email, national ID reference) outright. Where a record involves someone else too — a message thread, a job application, a rating — we remove your identity and content from it but keep what the other party (an employer, for example) has a legitimate reason to retain of their own side, such as the fact that an application existed and its outcome. This can't happen while a report or dispute involving your account is actively open, so it can't be used to remove evidence mid-investigation.
  • Blockchain entries themselves are never deleted — that's inherent to how a blockchain works, not a YouthChain choice. But since only a fingerprint of your file is ever stored there (see above), deleting the original file and its record here is what actually matters for your privacy: what remains on-chain cannot be traced back to you or your document by anyone, including YouthChain, once that's done.
